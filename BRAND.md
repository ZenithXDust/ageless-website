# Brand assets and setting up online profiles

## What I cannot do

I cannot create social media accounts for you. Signing up requires your phone,
your email and identity verification, and creating accounts in your name is not
something I should do on your behalf even if it were technically possible. This
environment also blocks those sites.

What I have done is make every image you need, so setting an account up is
uploading two files and pasting some text.

## The files

Everything is in the `brand/` folder.

| File | Size | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| `profile-light.png` | 1000 × 1000 | Profile picture. The mark on warm paper. Use this as the default. |
| `profile-dark.png` | 1000 × 1000 | Profile picture, reversed on green. Use where profile pictures sit on a white page and you want more presence. |
| `banner-linkedin.png` | 1584 × 396 | LinkedIn company page cover. |
| `share-card-1200x630.png` | 1200 × 630 | Facebook page cover, and the image that already appears when the website link is shared anywhere. |

Square profile images get cropped to a circle on most platforms. These are
padded so nothing important is cut off.

## Where to actually be, in order of value

Be honest about who you are selling to. Your buyer is a worried adult child
aged roughly 45 to 65. They are not looking for you on TikTok.

**1. Google Business Profile. Do this one first.**
It is free, it is what appears when somebody searches for help keeping a
parent at home in Ottawa, and it is where reviews accumulate. For a local service business it is
worth more than every social account combined. Set it up at
google.com/business. You will need to verify your address, which for a business
without a storefront means listing a service area rather than a location.

**2. Facebook page.**
Where your buyer actually is, and where local community groups discuss exactly
this problem. Use `profile-light.png` and `share-card-1200x630.png`.

**3. LinkedIn company page.**
Not for finding clients. For credibility when a social worker, discharge
planner or occupational therapist looks you up before referring somebody. Those
referrals are likely to be worth more than direct enquiries. Use
`banner-linkedin.png`.

**4. Instagram.** Only once you have photographs worth posting. An empty or
abandoned account is worse than no account.

## Text you can paste

**Short bio, under 160 characters:**

> Home technology chosen, installed and kept working, so older adults can stay
> in their own homes. Ottawa and Toronto. 437-457-5307

**Longer description:**

> Ageless helps older adults stay in their own homes instead of moving into a
> retirement residence.
>
> We do three things: we sell packages of technology chosen for a particular
> worry, at a published price, we install and set them up in the home, and we
> run an ongoing monthly service that keeps everything working and the family
> informed.
>
> We are not a home care agency and we do not send caregivers. We are not a
> device manufacturer. We choose the equipment, sell it, fit it and maintain
> it, and because we are not tied to one manufacturer we can tell you when a
> product is not right for the home.
>
> Founded by Amin Osman, who holds a BScN and CompTIA A+, Network+ and
> Security+ certifications, with experience in in-home care and crisis
> intervention.
>
> Serving Ottawa and the surrounding region, and Toronto and the Greater
> Toronto Area. Call 437-457-5307.

**Categories to choose, where a platform asks:** Home Automation Company,
Electronics Store, or Senior Care Service, in that order of accuracy. Avoid
anything with "medical" in the name. A retail category is now more accurate
than a care category.

## Two rules that carry over from the website

1. **Never call yourself a nurse, an RN, or registered.** Those are protected
   titles in Ontario. A BScN is a degree and can be stated. The title cannot.
2. **Never imply Ageless answers emergencies.** The 24/7 availability is
   technical support. If a social post gets shortened to "24/7 monitoring", fix
   it.

## Business cards

`print/ageless-business-card.pdf` is a two page, print-ready PDF. Page one is
the front, page two is the back.

- **Size:** 3.5 × 2 inches trimmed, which is standard North American.
- **Bleed:** the file is 3.75 × 2.25 inches, which includes the 0.125 inch bleed
  printers ask for. Tell them the file already has bleed.
- **What to order:** double sided, matte or soft touch rather than gloss. Matte
  suits the warm palette and is easier to read.
- **Colour:** the file is RGB. Most online printers convert it themselves. If a
  printer asks for CMYK, tell them and they will handle the conversion.

Anywhere that prints cards will accept this file as it is.
