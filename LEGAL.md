# Legal documents: what they are and what they are not

`terms.html` and `privacy.html` are complete, thorough documents written to
cover the real exposure this business has. They are far better than nothing and
better than most small business boilerplate, because they were written for what
Ageless actually does rather than copied from a template for a different trade.

They were not written by a lawyer, and they were not reviewed by one.

## The thing that must be said plainly

No document can eliminate legal or regulatory risk. Anyone who tells you
otherwise is selling something. In particular, terms of service cannot:

- override the Ontario Consumer Protection Act, which applies whatever a
  contract says
- exclude liability for negligence causing death or personal injury
- override PIPEDA obligations about consent, access and breach reporting
- authorise anything that professional regulation prohibits
- bind somebody who never actually agreed to them

What good terms do is set expectations, define scope, allocate risk where the
law permits it, and give you something to point at in a dispute. That is
genuinely valuable. It is not immunity.

## Why the exposure here is above average

This business carries more risk than a typical local service company:

1. **Vulnerable clients.** Older adults, sometimes with cognitive change.
   Consent and capacity are live issues on every job.
2. **Safety-critical expectations.** Families will believe the equipment keeps
   their parent safe. When something goes wrong, the gap between what they
   believed and what was promised is where a claim lives.
3. **Health-adjacent information.** Falls history, medication timing, mobility.
   Sensitive under PIPEDA, with breach reporting duties attached.
4. **A protected title nearby.** A BScN is a degree. Nurse, RN and registered
   are protected titles in Ontario and appear nowhere on this site, deliberately.
5. **Third-party equipment.** Somebody else's device fails in your client's
   home. Who the client sues first is not always who made it.
6. **You are now a retailer.** This is new, and it changes the shape of the
   risk rather than just its size. Ageless used to advise and install. It now
   buys goods, marks them up, and sells them to consumers in their own homes.
   Selling goods carries statutory obligations that advising does not, and
   several of them cannot be contracted out of.

## What to have a lawyer check, in priority order

1. **The liability limit in section 12 of the terms.** Limits of this kind are
   often read narrowly by Ontario courts, especially against consumers. Ask
   whether it holds and how to strengthen it.
2. **Whether the "not an emergency service" language is strong enough**, and
   whether it needs to be a signed acknowledgement at the assessment rather than
   a page on a website. This is the single largest exposure.
3. **Consent and capacity.** What to do when a family insists and the resident
   cannot meaningfully agree. Ask about a written consent form and what to
   accept as proof of a power of attorney.
4. **Cancellation and refund terms** against the Consumer Protection Act,
   including the rules on direct agreements signed in somebody's home, which
   carry specific cooling-off rights in Ontario.
5. **The privacy policy** against PIPEDA, particularly cross-border storage and
   breach reporting.
6. **Insurance.** Ask about commercial general liability and errors and
   omissions cover. Insurance, not contract wording, is what actually absorbs a
   claim. Nothing about insurance appears on the site because it has not been
   confirmed.

### Added when the business became a retailer

These four were not live issues under the old advisory model. They are now.
They are listed as questions, not answers, because answering them is what the
lawyer is for.

7. **Consumer protection rules on goods sold in somebody's home.** Ontario
   treats an agreement made in a consumer's home differently from one made in
   a shop or online, and there are specific rules about what such an agreement
   must contain and what the consumer may do afterwards. Every Ageless sale
   happens in exactly that setting. Ask what the agreement has to say, in
   writing, at the point of sale, and what cancellation right attaches to it
   regardless of what `terms.html` says. The terms currently point at those
   rights in general terms without stating them, deliberately, because stating
   a right wrongly is worse than pointing at it.
8. **Warranty and returns on equipment.** Ageless passes through the
   manufacturer's warranty and adds none of its own. Ask whether a seller can
   do that in Ontario, what implied conditions attach to goods sold by a
   business whatever the contract says, and whether "ordered for your home
   specifically, so it may not be returnable" is enforceable against a
   consumer. Section 7 of the terms is written on the assumption that it is
   roughly right, which is not the same as knowing.
9. **Liability when a third party device fails.** The most likely bad day for
   this business: a family bought a package, a sensor made by somebody else
   did not fire, and somebody was hurt. As an installer Ageless was one step
   removed from the manufacturer. As the seller of the goods it is not. Ask
   how that changes exposure, and whether the limitation in section 12 does
   anything useful in that scenario.
10. **The scope of practice sentence itself.** It was rewritten when the
    business changed and has not been reviewed. It now reads:

    > Ageless selects, supplies, installs and maintains technology in the
    > home, and provides residential technology and IT systems advice. Our
    > services do not constitute regulated nursing care, medical diagnosis, or
    > physical therapy.

    The second sentence is unchanged from the reviewed-by-nobody original and
    is the one doing the protective work. The first sentence is new. Ask
    whether it accurately and sufficiently describes what is being sold, and
    whether the second sentence still covers a business that now sells goods
    rather than assessments.

    **A third sentence has been drafted and deliberately not published.** It
    covers the gap the first two do not reach, which is monitoring and
    emergency response:

    > Ageless does not monitor its clients, and does not receive or act on
    > alerts on their behalf. Alerts from installed equipment are routed to
    > emergency services and to the contacts the household chooses.

    This is on no page. It is here for the lawyer to rule on. The argument for
    adding it is that "not an emergency service" is the single largest
    exposure this business has, and the footer disclaimer currently says
    nothing about it, leaving that work entirely to `terms.html` section 3 and
    to a callout on two pages. The argument against is that three sentences in
    a footer on every page is where people stop reading, and diluting the
    scope sentence may weaken it. That is a judgement for somebody qualified
    to make it.

## Terms confirmed by the owner

- The first conversation is a free phone call of about twenty minutes. There is
  no charge and no obligation. The $249 assessment is no longer sold.
- Cancel or move any appointment at any time before it, at no charge.
- The monthly service can be cancelled at any time: no notice period, no fee,
  and the unused part of the month is refunded.
- Response time: within one business day.
- Package prices are fixed and published. Network work is a separately named
  item with its own fixed published price. There are no "from" prices and no
  ranges.
- One monthly service price for every customer, whatever they bought.

These are deliberately generous, chosen on the reasoning that a service which is
hard to leave is a service anxious families hesitate to start. The commercial
risk is real but small: the exposure is one wasted trip and part of one month.

## Terms still assumed, not confirmed

- Monthly service billed in advance
- Package price and any network charge payable as agreed before work begins
- Client records kept for seven years after the service ends
- Non-client enquiries deleted within twelve months
- 30 days notice **from Ageless** if we end a monthly service

Newly assumed, and worth confirming before anything is sold:

- That there is no deposit, and nothing is payable before the order is placed.
  The terms do not mention one because nobody has said there is one.
- Whether a no-show for a booked installation is chargeable. The old terms
  allowed charging half the assessment fee for a no-show. There is no fee to
  halve now, so the clause was removed rather than replaced with a number
  nobody has decided. If you want a no-show charge, it has to be decided and
  written down.
- Whether the network charge is refundable if the package is cancelled after
  the network work is done but before the equipment is installed.

## Also outstanding

- **The exact registered legal name.** The business is incorporated, but the
  spelling was given verbally and could not be transcribed reliably. Until it is
  confirmed in writing, the footer and the terms both say "Ageless". A wrong
  legal name in a contract term is worse than none.
