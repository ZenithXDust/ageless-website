Why a nurse walks your home

Adapting a house for safety shouldn’t mean it stops feeling like home. We focus on practical, high-impact changes that protect your mobility and your independence.

A clinical perspective

A contractor looks at walls and plumbing. A Registered Nurse looks at how the body moves through a space — how you rise from a chair, turn in a shower, or take a stair in low light — and where that interaction creates risk.

Practical, low-cost changes

Most of what makes a home safer is inexpensive: a grab bar in exactly the right place, a seat at the right height, motion lighting along the route you walk at night. We recommend what earns its place and skip what doesn’t.

A plan you stay in control of

Within 48 hours you receive a written action plan, sorted by priority, with costs and timeframes. You decide what happens, and when. We never take over.

How the assessment works

One visit, about two hours, conducted with you — and, if you wish, your family.

  1. The pre-assessment conversation

    Before we inspect a single room, we sit down to understand your daily routines, your goals, and what independence means to you. You are the expert on your life; we are here to support it.

  2. The clinical walk-through

    We move through the home at your pace — measuring clearances, testing lighting, and assessing how you transition between sitting, standing, and walking. We concentrate on entryways, stairways, bathrooms, and the kitchen.

  3. The priority action plan

    A clear summary matrix: immediate priorities, 30-day recommendations, and longer-term considerations. Yours to act on directly, or to hand to a trusted contractor.

Where falls actually happen

Four zones account for most serious falls at home. They get the closest attention in every assessment.

  • Bathrooms

    Wet, hard, and enclosed. Grab bars at the tub threshold and along the wash wall, a seat at a workable height, a handheld shower, non-slip footing, and water set below scald temperature.

  • Kitchens

    Everyday items between knee and shoulder height so no one climbs a chair. Front-mounted controls, task lighting with no shadow on the work surface, and a clear run for heavy pots.

  • Stairways & hallways

    Continuous, firm handrails that extend past the top and bottom steps. Contrast on the stair edge, even light with switches at both ends, and a lit path from bedroom to bathroom.

  • Entryways & thresholds

    A step-free way in and out where possible, thresholds bevelled below half an inch, and motion-activated lighting that covers the path, the steps, and the lock.
