Privacy policy

What we collect, why, who sees it, and how to get it back or have it deleted. Last updated 31 August 2026.

The short version

This website collects nothing about you. To do the actual work, we need to know things about a household, some of which is sensitive. We use it to do the job and for nothing else. We never sell it. The person living in the home can see it, correct it, or have it deleted.

1. Who is responsible

Ageless, operated by Amin Osman, serving Ottawa and Toronto, Ontario. Amin is the person accountable for privacy. Call 437-457-5307.

We handle personal information under Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act, known as PIPEDA, and under Ontario law where it applies.

2. This website

The website sets no cookies, runs no analytics, carries no advertising and embeds nothing from social media. There is no tracking of any kind.

The booking page keeps your choices inside your own browser only, so it can show them back to you. Nothing is transmitted to Ageless until you decide to pick up the phone.

Our host keeps ordinary web server records, in the same way as every website you visit.

3. What we collect when you become a client

To assess a home and install equipment in it, we need:

  • names and contact details for the household and the family members involved
  • the address and the layout of the home
  • details of the daily routine of the person living there
  • relevant health and mobility information, for example a history of falls, medication timing, or difficulty with stairs
  • emergency contacts and who should be alerted, in what order
  • information about the internet connection and any equipment already installed

Some of this is health-related, which makes it sensitive. We collect only what we need to do the work, and we ask before we collect it.

4. What we use it for

  • carrying out the assessment and writing the report
  • recommending, sourcing, installing and configuring equipment
  • providing the monthly service and technical support
  • routing alerts to the people the household has nominated
  • billing, and keeping the records a business is required to keep

We do not use it for anything else. We do not sell it, rent it, or trade it. We do not use it to advertise to you.

5. Consent, and who gives it

We ask for consent before collecting information, and we explain what it is for.

The person who lives in the home consents to monitoring in that home. A family member paying for the service cannot give that consent on their behalf, unless they hold valid legal authority such as a power of attorney or a guardianship order and show it to us.

Consent can be withdrawn at any time. We will stop, and remove or disable the equipment. Some information may have to be kept where the law requires it, and we will say so if that applies.

6. Who else sees it

Only these, and only what they need:

  • Equipment and monitoring companies. Setting up a device usually means creating an account with the company that makes it. They receive what is needed for that, and they have their own privacy policies, which we will identify for you.
  • The people you nominate. Alerts and updates go to the contacts the household chooses, in the order it chooses.
  • Emergency services. If equipment generates an emergency alert, relevant details may be passed to 911 or to responders.
  • Where the law requires it. For example, in response to a court order.

Some of these companies operate outside Canada, which means information may be stored in another country and be accessible to that country's authorities under its laws. We will tell you when this applies to a device we are recommending.

7. How it is protected

Records are kept on encrypted devices with access controls, and accounts use multi-factor authentication where the service supports it. Access is limited to those who need it, which today means Amin. Paper notes taken during a visit are transferred to the digital record and then destroyed.

No safeguard is perfect. If a breach occurs that creates a real risk of significant harm, we will notify the people affected and the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, as the law requires.

8. How long we keep it

We keep client records for as long as the service continues, and then for seven years, which is the period Canadian tax and business records rules generally require. After that they are securely destroyed.

Enquiries that do not become clients are deleted within twelve months.

9. Your rights

  • Ask what information we hold about you, and get a copy.
  • Ask us to correct anything wrong.
  • Withdraw consent, subject to what the law requires us to keep.
  • Ask us to delete your information when the service ends.
  • Complain, to us first and to the Privacy Commissioner if we do not resolve it.

Call 437-457-5307 and ask for Amin. We will respond within 30 days, which is the period PIPEDA sets.

10. Complaints beyond us

If you are not satisfied with how we have handled a privacy concern, you can contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. They take complaints from the public about how organisations handle personal information.

11. Changes

We may update this page. The date at the top shows when it last changed. If a change materially affects existing clients, we will tell them directly.
