How it works

Three parts: an assessment, an installation, and an ongoing monthly service. Here is what each one actually involves, so there are no surprises.

Step one: the assessment visit

We arrange a time that suits the household, including evenings and weekends. We walk through the home with whoever wants to be there. If your parent would rather do it without family present, that is completely fine, and it often goes better.

We look at:

  • the kitchen, and how cooking actually happens day to day
  • stairs, thresholds, and the routes walked most often
  • the bathroom
  • lighting, especially between the bed and the bathroom at night
  • doors, locks, and who currently has a key
  • water, heat, and anything that could leak or fail unnoticed
  • the internet connection, because nearly all of this depends on it
  • the daily routine, which usually matters more than the building

We do not bring a catalogue to the visit, and we do not sell anything on the day.

What the written report says

After the visit you get a written report. Plain language, no jargon, written so that it can be read by the whole family and argued about if necessary.

It sets out:

  • what we found, ordered by how much it actually matters
  • what we would recommend, and why that and not something else
  • what it would cost
  • what you could do yourselves for nothing

That last one is not a throwaway. Some of the most useful changes in a home cost nothing at all, and we would rather tell you than sell you something instead.

Step two: installation day

We order the equipment, bring it, and install it. We set up the accounts and the apps, test everything, and show whoever needs to know how it works. We take the packaging away with us.

Nothing gets installed that was not in the report and agreed to first. If we get there and something does not suit the house after all, we stop and talk to you rather than pressing on.

Step three: the monthly service

Technology that nobody maintains stops working, and it usually stops quietly. A dead battery does not announce itself. This is the part that most families do not think to ask about, and it is the part that decides whether any of it is still working in a year.

The monthly service covers:

  • checking that the system is genuinely running, not just installed
  • batteries, replacements and repairs
  • software updates and security patches
  • reconfiguration when the internet or the phone changes
  • a regular plain-language update, so the family is not left guessing

Technical support is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, remotely or in person.

What Ageless is not

Ageless is not an emergency service.

If something happens, the system contacts emergency services and the people on the household's contact list. The 24 hour availability is technical support: if a device fails at two in the morning, that is what we handle. In a medical emergency, call 911.

We do not send caregivers. Ageless is not a home care agency. We do not provide personal care or nursing care, and we will happily point you towards people who do.

We do not manufacture equipment. We choose it, install it, and keep it working. That means we can recommend against something when it is not right for the home, which a company selling its own product cannot really do.
