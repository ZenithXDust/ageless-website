Clinical safety framework

Four zones, assessed room by room.

Most serious falls at home happen in a small number of places, during a small number of movements. Ageless concentrates on four priority zones — and on the specific, measurable standards that make each one safer without making the house feel clinical.

How the zones are prioritised

Each zone below expands to the standards we check on site. Findings are graded against the property, never the person, and every recommendation is yours to accept, modify, or decline. Cost bands are planning estimates: $ under $25 · $$ $25–75 · $$$ $75–200 · $$$$ over $200 or professional install.

Zone 1 Bathrooms

Wet, hard, and enclosed — the highest-consequence room in the home. Risk peaks during transfers: stepping over a tub wall, lowering to and rising from the toilet, turning while wet. We assess every bathroom the person uses and ask for a demonstration of the tub or shower transfer.

Wet & slip hazards

  • Floor is not slippery when wet; any bath mat has full non-slip backing and lies flat.
  • Tub or shower floor has texture, adhesive treads, or a full-contact suction mat.
  • Bright, even light at the mirror and inside the shower; a night light for overnight trips.

Equip Rubber-backed low-pile mat ($) · adhesive treads / full-suction mat ($) · non-slip floor treatment ($–$$) · damp-rated shower light ($$).

Grab bar placement — tub & toilet

  • Tub: a vertical bar at the threshold for the step in and out; a horizontal bar on the long wall for standing balance; a second bar where needed.
  • Toilet: a dedicated grab bar or floor-mounted safety frame within reach — towel bars and the tank are not load-bearing.
  • Spec: 1¼–1½″ diameter · 1½″ wall clearance · anchored into studs or rated heavy-duty anchors · rated ≥ 250 lb. Never a suction-cup bar as a load-bearing hold.

Equip Vertical + horizontal grab bars ($–$$ each + install $$) · floor-mounted toilet safety frame, no drilling ($$).

Handheld showerheads

  • Head on a slide bar with a 5–6 ft hose, so the person can wash seated and rinse without standing or turning.
  • Paired with a shower chair or transfer bench: rubber tips, adjustable height, drains well; back and arm rests if transfers are effortful.
  • Seat height 17–19″ to the top of the seat; feet flat, knees roughly level with hips.

Equip Handheld shower + slide bar ($–$$, no plumber) · shower chair ($$) / transfer bench ($$–$$$) · raised toilet seat ($–$$).

Scald prevention

  • Water heater set to a safe maximum — no hotter than 120°F.
  • Single-lever or thermostatic mixing valve so temperature can’t swing suddenly.
  • Controls reachable from a seated position, without leaning across the stream.

Equip Anti-scald / thermostatic valve ($$–$$$) · lever handle retrofit ($–$$).

Zone 2 Kitchens

Standing tasks, hot surfaces, and two-handed carrying combine here, so a fall in the kitchen often involves a burn or a scald as well. The aim is to keep everything used regularly within an easy, balanced reach and to remove the need to climb, stretch, or carry across the room.

Reach ergonomics

  • Everyday dishes, food, and cookware sit between knee and shoulder height.
  • Nothing used weekly requires a step stool or a deep bend to the floor.
  • Where reaching high is unavoidable, a stable step stool with a high hand-hold is available — never a chair.

Equip Pull-down wall-cabinet shelf ($$$) · lazy-Susans / drawer organisers ($) · step stool with a support handle ($$) · reacher ($).

Task lighting

  • Dedicated light falls on the stove, the sink, and the main counter.
  • No shadow cast on the work surface by the person’s own body.
  • Glare from bare bulbs and windows is controlled.

Equip Stick-on or plug-in under-cabinet LED strips ($–$$).

Stove control access

  • Controls are at the front, so there is no reaching across a hot burner.
  • Markings are legible; the person can reliably confirm the burners are off.
  • A photoelectric smoke alarm sits 10+ ft from the range; a fire extinguisher the person can lift and operate is within reach.

Equip Large-print control overlays ($) · stove shut-off timer or smart plug ($–$$) · 2–5 lb extinguisher ($$).

Movement & carrying

  • Landing counter space beside both the stove and the fridge; a clear run to slide heavy pots rather than carry them.
  • Non-slip footing with no loose mat at the sink; spills show against the floor colour.
  • A phone or call device is reachable in case of a burn or a fall.

Equip Rolling kitchen cart ($$) · anti-fatigue mat with a bevelled, non-slip edge ($–$$).

Zone 3 Stairways & hallways

Descending a stair in low light is one of the highest-risk moments in any home, and the night route between bedroom and bathroom is a close second. Continuous support for the hand and a continuously lit, level path are the two things that matter most.

Continuous dual handrails

  • A graspable handrail runs the full length of every flight, on both sides where possible — the descending side matters most.
  • The rail extends horizontally about 12″ beyond the top and bottom steps and returns toward the wall at each end.
  • Spec: round profile ~1¼–2″ · 34–38″ above the nosings · 1½″ wall clearance · anchored into framing, no movement under full weight.

Equip Second handrail ($$$ + install) · bolt-on rail extension / return fittings ($–$$ per end + install $$) · re-anchor into studs ($$).

Step-edge contrast

  • A contrasting strip on the nosing, at minimum on the top and bottom steps.
  • Uniform tread depth and riser height — variation under 3/8″ across the flight.
  • Any single “surprise” step is flagged and marked.

Equip High-contrast anti-slip nosing tape ($); photoluminescent for night visibility.

Continuous pathway night-lighting

  • Even light along the whole route with no dark stretch.
  • Switches at both the top and bottom of the stairs and by room doorways.
  • A lit path from the bedroom to the bathroom at ankle height for night use.

Equip Three-way switch ($$ electrician) · plug-in motion night lights, one every 6–8 ft ($ each) · motion stair lights ($–$$).

Clear, even pathways

  • Hallways ≥ 36″ clear, ≥ 32″ at the tightest pinch point.
  • Consistent flooring; transitions flush or bevelled (≤ ½″).
  • Throw rugs removed or secured flat on all edges; no cords across the path; nothing stored on the stairs; landings clear of a door swing.

Equip Rug tape / non-slip pad ($) · cord raceways ($) · stud-mounted wall rail on a long unsupported wall ($$).

Zone 4 Entryways & thresholds

The entrance combines a change in surface, a change in elevation, weather underfoot, and the need to manage keys and a door at the same time. A step-free route in and out — used daily, not just “available” — removes the hardest part.

Zero-step entries

  • At least one entrance is usable without steps, now or if a mobility device is anticipated.
  • Where steps remain: a firm handrail on at least one side (both preferred), uniform step height, visible edges.
  • Path clear width ≥ 36″; landing at the door ≥ 36″ deep, ideally 60″ × 60″ with a device.

Equip Aluminium threshold ramp / wedge ($$–$$$) · handrail addition ($$$ + install) · non-slip tread strips ($).

Threshold beveling

  • Entry thresholds are ≤ ½″ or bevelled — no raised strip to catch a toe or a wheel.
  • Flooring is consistent on both sides of the doorway.
  • Interior thresholds between rooms meet the same standard.

Equip Rubber or aluminium bevelled threshold ($$–$$$) · transition wedge for a single high sill ($$).

Exterior pathway lighting

  • Light covers the path, the steps, and the lock itself.
  • Switchable from inside and outside; motion-activated so hands stay free.
  • House number is visible from the street, day and night, for responders.

Equip Motion-activated LED porch and path lights, solar or wired ($–$$ each) · reflective or illuminated house numbers ($–$$).

Door operation & arrival

  • A lock the person operates easily — lever or keyless rather than a small knob or key.
  • A level place to stand, and to set a bag down, while unlocking.
  • Mail and parcels can be collected without taking a step one-handed.

Equip Lever handle ($–$$) · keypad / keyless deadbolt ($$–$$$) · a small shelf or bench by the door ($–$$).

This framework describes environmental standards only. Where balance, strength, vision, cognition, or a recent fall suggest a clinical need, we recommend follow-up with a physician or a physical or occupational therapist.
