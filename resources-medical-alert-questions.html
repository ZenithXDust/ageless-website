Five questions to ask before buying a medical alert button

The device matters much less than whether it gets worn. These five questions sort out the useful products from the ones that end up in a drawer.

Medical alert buttons are the most bought and least worn piece of safety equipment there is. The failure is rarely technical. It is almost always that the device did not fit the life of the person expected to wear it. Ask these five before spending anything.

1. Will it be worn in the shower?

Bathrooms are where a large share of falls happen, and it is the place a pendant is most likely to be taken off. Ask whether the device is genuinely waterproof, not "water resistant", and whether it is comfortable enough to keep on while washing. If the honest answer is that it will come off, then the pendant is not protecting the room where protection matters most, and you need a different approach for that room.

2. Does it work outside the house?

Many buttons only work within range of a base unit in the hallway. That is fine for somebody who is mostly indoors and useless for somebody who gardens, walks to the shops, or takes the bin out on a frosty morning. Ask specifically about the back garden, the driveway and the garage, and test it in those places rather than taking the box's word for it.

3. What happens if they cannot press it?

This is the question that gets missed. A person who is unconscious, or who has landed on the arm wearing the button, cannot press anything. Automatic fall detection works without a press. It is not perfect, and it does generate the occasional false alarm, but it covers the exact situation a manual button cannot. Ask whether the device has it, whether it costs extra, and whether it can be turned off if the false alarms become a nuisance.

4. Who is on the other end, and what do they do?

Some services call an emergency number. Some call a family member first. Some route to a monitoring centre with staff. These are genuinely different products and the price difference is not always a guide to which is which. Ask what happens in the first sixty seconds after the button is pressed, and ask what happens if nobody answers.

5. What does it cost per month, and what happens if you stop?

Most of these devices are cheap to buy and carry a monthly fee. Ask what the fee covers, whether the hardware still works if the subscription lapses, and what the cancellation terms are. A device that turns into a paperweight the month you stop paying is a different purchase from one you own.

The question underneath all five

Would this person actually wear this, every day, without being reminded? If the answer is no, the specification does not matter. Better to accept that early and look at something that does not depend on being worn, than to spend the money and discover it six months later in a bedside drawer.
