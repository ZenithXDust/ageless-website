The Unbroken Line

Ageless
Ottawa and Toronto

One stroke, never lifted. It climbs the wall, crosses the roof, comes down the far side, then sweeps up and over the whole house and back to where it began. The bottom stays open, so it reads as a doorway rather than an enclosure. That is the right message for a business whose entire point is that nobody is being shut in anywhere.

My recommendation

Take B, and keep C in reserve

I built the same tests for all four and then read the results instead of my own preference. Here is what they actually show.

  • C is the most legible at 24 pixels. A solid shape always wins that test. Check the favicon row on each.
  • B is close behind and keeps its shape, because it is only three strokes and a dot.
  • A is the weakest small. The ring and the roof close up on each other and it becomes a blob. It is the best looking at full size and I would still not choose it.

So why B rather than C, when C tests better? Because C is the most generic idea of the four. A house inside a circle is what half the home services companies in Ottawa already use. B carries one specific idea, the light left on, and it is also the letter A, so it works as a monogram on an app icon, a lapel pin, or a return address label. Legibility is a floor to clear, not a prize to win, and B clears it comfortably.

C is worth keeping for physical things, where a solid shape survives vinyl cutting, embroidery, and being read from across a parking lot. Plenty of small service businesses run a line mark online and a solid seal on the van. That is a decision for later, not now.

All four are drawn as SVG, which means they are a few lines of text rather than an image file. They stay sharp at any size, they cost nothing to load, and I can recolour or resize any of them in seconds when you change your mind.
